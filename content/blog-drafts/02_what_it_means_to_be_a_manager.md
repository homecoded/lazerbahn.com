# What is being a manager all about?

Over many years, I worked my way up from developer to manager. That's how I learned to deal with different levels of 
the power hierarchy. On my way up, I developed ideas on how to do things. At the time, I felt that everything would 
be different (better) if I were just the boss and could make the decisions. Right? Yes!

When I was finally in charge of my own little world (a nice office with a few developers and a project manager), 
I found that being a good boss wasn't as easy as it seemed when I looked up.

Much of my work there was invisible to the team. It was mostly about creating a positive and supportive 
environment where your team can thrive and do their best work. That means making sure your team members feel 
welcomed, valued and appreciated, and that their ideas and concerns are heard and addressed. And making sure they
are shielded from the pressure from upper management or customers.

One of the most important roles of a leader is to ensure that the team can perform to its full potential. 
This can be hard, exhausting work that often goes on in the background as you work to identify and remove 
bottlenecks, inefficiencies, or other obstacles to your team's success. This may include providing training 
and resources, fostering communication and collaboration, and helping to resolve conflicts or other issues.

Dealing with interpersonal challenges within a team can be particularly stressful, often requiring a great deal of 
emotional energy and attention. It can be especially difficult to put these types of issues behind you when you go 
home at the end of the day, as they can continue to weigh on you and interfere with your personal life.

Coping with interpersonal problems can be especially difficult when they involve complex emotions and relationships, 
and it is easy to feel overwhelmed or drained by the effort it takes to manage them. It's important to remember that 
taking care of yourself and setting boundaries for work is essential to avoiding burnout and maintaining a healthy 
work-life balance. This includes, for example, taking time to relax and unwind after work or seeking support from 
friends, family or a psychologist when needed.

Having a clear vision for the team you lead and following through on those plans can be difficult and requires good 
planning and perseverance. As a leader, it is important that you have a clear vision of where you want to take your 
team and that you are able to communicate that vision to your team members. This may include setting long-term goals 
and objectives and developing strategies and plans to achieve them.

However, it is not enough to just have a vision. It is also important to be able to follow through and follow through 
on your plans. This requires good planning and organizational skills, as well as the ability to stay focused and 
motivated during difficult times. It may also include adapting your plans as needed to market changes or other 
external factors and persevering in the face of challenges or setbacks.

A good leader may not always be visible, but their efforts are noticeable to the team in the form of a positive 
and productive work environment. A bad leader, on the other hand, will always be noticed, and their negative 
impact on the team will also be felt. As a leader, it is important that you strive to be a positive and supportive 
presence for your team and do everything you can to help them succeed.
